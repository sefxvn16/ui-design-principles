# Touch: tablet, mobile

Read when the product uses touch and the task touches controls, target sizes, or layout that depends on screen size. The shared rules in `SKILL.md` still apply; this file adds only what changes the layout. For a tablet with a mouse or keyboard, also read `desktop.en.md`.

- **Declare the posture:** record screen orientation, the hand holding the device, the touching finger, and screen size before placing controls by hand position. Apply only research or position rules from the same posture; do not use the "thumb zone" from one-handed portrait studies for a landscape screen held with two hands. When the target device or posture changes, recheck the placement decisions based on the old posture.
- **Target size:** 44×44 (WCAG 2.5.5 level AAA; Apple HIG 44 pt) or about 48 dp, roughly 9 mm, spaced about 8 px apart (web.dev, Accessible tap targets). When the values differ, follow `DESIGN.md`; if absent, state the values with their conditions and ask when the choice changes the layout. When comparing with thresholds in mm, convert CSS px to mm for the device: mm = CSS px × (screen width in mm ÷ viewport width in CSS px).
- **Invisible hit area:** when the visible element is smaller than the threshold, extend an invisible hit area around it; do not enlarge rows or images in ways that break proportion and rhythm. Hit areas of different targets do not overlap and do not create scrollbars in their container. Failure: setting `min-height` to the threshold inflates the whole table.
- **Targets near the screen edge:** either touch the edge or keep a clear distance from it; avoid the in-between band. Reference value: touch points drift within about 6.4 mm of the edge (Kasahara, Yamanaka, Miyashita 2026: 15 participants, one hand holding, index finger of the other hand touching, Pixel 6a); other postures use it only as a reference.
- **Rotation and safe areas:** portrait and landscape are two layouts rearranged by priority (E), not one rotated layout. Essential controls and information stay within the safe area reported by the platform (notch, rounded corners, system bars; on the web, `env(safe-area-inset-*)`).
- **Occlusion:** the finger and hand cover the touched area and its surroundings; information needed for a decision and the feedback of an action are not placed directly under the path of the hand. The on-screen keyboard does not cover the field being typed in or the continue button.
- **No hover:** required actions do not depend on hover; long-press is never the only path.
- **Checks:** measure at the smallest viewport of the target device (usually a phone in landscape) and one larger viewport. Measuring in a browser emulator does not prove touch accuracy; if not tested on a real device, say so.
